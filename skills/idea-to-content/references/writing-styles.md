# Writing styles and social openings

Use these controls when drafting or changing the voice of audience-facing copy. They are starting points, not restrictions on natural-language requests. Attention and a useful payoff matter across styles; there is no word list or setting that guarantees virality.

## Voice controls

Choose one primary style. An optional second style adds a lighter influence: `Educational + Entertaining` keeps the explanation central and uses humour to help it land. Do not mix every preset. When specific user instructions conflict with a preset, follow the specific instructions. If two equally specific requests cannot coexist, explain the necessary tradeoff briefly or ask only if it prevents a useful draft.

| Style | Choices that should change the writing | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engaging (default) | Open on a recognisable friction, curiosity, or desire; move quickly into a concrete insight or demonstration; end with a payoff that answers the opening. | Empty teasers, a generic motivational speech, or unrelated oddity merely to attract attention. |
| Educational | Show an understandable example early, unpack the useful mechanism or steps, then give a practical application. Use a conversational explanation at the audience's level. | An agenda, textbook introduction, or a list of tips without showing what they mean. |
| Entertaining | Use a relevant scene, comic observation, playful comparison, or clearly hypothetical dialogue. Build a small setup and payoff around the real topic. | Random jokes, forced slang, humiliation, or an invented personal story presented as true. |
| Emotional | Name a specific feeling or recognisable moment, show its stakes, and move toward a believable insight or possibility. Let concrete language carry the emotion. | Guilt, melodrama, invented hardship/testimony, or claims about what every viewer feels. |
| Professional | Lead with a clear observation or implication; use precise, measured language and a useful conclusion. | Forced banter, emoji, exaggerated certainty, or stiff corporate filler. |
| Custom | Follow the user's description and supplied writing samples; identify observable features such as sentence rhythm, vocabulary, directness, and humour. | Copying facts, credentials, anecdotes, or promises from a style sample into the user's voice. |

**Energy** controls delivery: `Calm` uses measured pacing and space; `Balanced` mixes concise lines with connected explanation; `Bold` uses a decisive opening, tighter pacing, and stronger emphasis. Bold changes expression, not certainty or the strength of evidence. Calm copy still needs a reason to keep reading.

**Wording** controls language: `Plain` uses simple, literal phrasing; `Conversational` uses natural spoken phrasing and contractions; `Polished` uses precise, fluent sentences suited to the intended audience. Do not inject slang, profanity, jargon, or emoji just because energy is Bold or style is Entertaining. Use them when requested or supported by a relevant writing sample.

Preserve explicit audience, platform, language, accessibility, length, and brand requirements. A goal describes what the content should accomplish; these controls describe how it sounds. A playful product launch still introduces the supplied product.

## Build attention into the copy

Start at the interesting moment. Choose an opening grounded in the audience and subject: a specific problem they recognise, a visible before/after example supported by the brief, a consequence they care about, an unresolved question the content actually answers, or a useful demonstration. Make the topic clear early; do not require an explanation of why an unrelated analogy matters.

For scripts, put this in the first spoken sentence. For posts, make the first line work without the internal title. Avoid greetings, agendas, and labels such as “Hook” inside finished copy. “You need to hear this” or “This changes everything” adds little without a specific, supportable point.

Give the audience something worthwhile early, then develop it. A useful shape is a recognisable moment, a concrete example or insight, and a satisfying resolution. Adapt that shape to the user's request rather than forcing every draft into a formula. Keep short videos around one main takeaway unless the requested scope needs more. Each beat should add an example, explanation, consequence, or action instead of restating the promise. A relevant story, demonstration, or comparison can carry advice more naturally than repeated commands.

Keep curiosity honest. The body must fulfil the opening's promise. Do not manufacture secrets, personal outcomes, consensus, statistics, urgency, or controversy. Avoid “watch until the end” as a substitute for value. A call to action should follow naturally from the payoff and should not hold the useful part hostage.

Generate hooks that could actually introduce the selected draft. If an alternative needs a small transition edit, make that clear in guidance; do not offer hooks that promise different content. Finish the selected draft so it needs no assembly.

## Review before returning

- **Audience relevance:** would the intended viewer recognise the situation or benefit from the opening alone?
- **Progression:** does the body deliver a concrete example or insight and keep adding information?
- **Voice:** is the chosen style apparent in structure and rhythm, not just the heading? Do energy and wording match?
- **Payoff:** does the ending fulfil the opening and leave something useful, memorable, or emotionally coherent?
- **Fidelity:** are supplied facts, required steps, tone overrides, scope, and copy boundaries intact? Is any story clearly supplied or hypothetical?

Revise substantive weaknesses before returning. Assess audience performance only from observed results; do not invent retention scores or label an untested draft “proven viral.”
